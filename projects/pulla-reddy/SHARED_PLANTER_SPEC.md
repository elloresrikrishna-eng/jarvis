# Shared Planter Specification | V02 and V06

## Authority and scope

This document is the single authoritative planter definition for the curved built-in seating zone shown in V02 (source PDF page 3) and V06 (source PDF page 7). Both views depict the same physical seating installation from different cameras. They must use one shared planter, not two similar interpretations.

The planter is a permanent architectural addition. It must be physically present from Layer 00 onward in both views, including every later cumulative layer and final image. It is unlit in Layer 00. This specification does not authorize rendering, lane advancement, or approval.

## Locked architectural geometry

- Form one continuous, built-in planting trough immediately behind the curved seater back, on the wall side of the existing timber-capped tiled backrest.
- Follow the existing seater's full compound curve as a parallel offset. The planter must read as purpose-made joinery belonging to the seater, never as freestanding pots, separate boxes, or an unrelated landscape strip.
- Begin and end with the physical extents of the curved built-in seater. Terminations must be clean and flush with the seater end conditions. Do not continue the planter onto unrelated counters, straight walls, door zones, or loose-furniture areas.
- Keep a consistent trough width and rim level along the curve. Use a visually narrow trough, approximately one third of the visible seat depth, sufficient for a continuous planting bed without consuming table or sitting clearance.
- Set the planter rim slightly above the existing backrest timber cap, approximately one cap thickness. The backrest, seat platform, tiled faces, timber caps, tables, chairs, circulation clearances, and wall geometry remain unchanged and visible.
- Use the source geometry as the dimensional authority. Do not invent a different radius, stepped profile, raised island, new wall, or bulky planter volume to make the planting more prominent.

## Material language and detailing

- Finish the planter's visible outer face and rim in the same dark warm timber language as the existing seater cap and seat surface.
- Keep the profile refined and quiet: a slim timber rim, concealed waterproof inner trough, no visible plastic liner, brackets, drainage fittings, soil spill, decorative metal band, or contrasting border.
- Where only the rim is visible behind foliage, retain enough continuous timber edge to make the shared curved geometry legible in both cameras.
- Preserve the store palette. Do not introduce black, white, glossy metallic, stone, concrete, or terracotta-tiled planter variants.

## Planting composition

- Use one consistent species palette in both views: restrained, shade-tolerant tropical foliage with predominantly medium and small matte green leaves. Suitable visual character is a controlled mix comparable to compact philodendron, pothos, and fern forms, without identifying individual specimens as different design features.
- Planting height measured above the planter rim must vary gently between approximately 350 mm and 650 mm. The average foliage mass should remain below roughly one third of the clear wall height above the backrest.
- Maintain medium density: enough overlap to read as one continuous planted ribbon, while retaining small pockets of negative space between clusters so individual leaf silhouettes and later shadow play remain legible.
- Establish a calm repeating rhythm of low, medium, medium-high, and medium clusters along the curve. Vary adjacent plants subtly to avoid a hedge-like top line, but do not create isolated feature plants or a random jungle effect.
- Keep the foliage envelope inside the planter footprint. Leaves may overlap the rim naturally but must not obstruct seating positions, table surfaces, chairs, circulation, wall shelves, or the camera.
- Use natural deep-to-medium greens with restrained variation. No flowers, coloured foliage, palms, trees, oversized leaves, hanging vines, topiary, or decorative gravel visible from either view.

## Cross-view identity lock

- Build or define the planter once in the shared scene geometry, then render that same object and plant layout from the V02 and V06 cameras.
- The curve, endpoints, trough width, rim level, timber finish, plant cluster order, plant heights, density, and leaf character are immutable across V02 and V06.
- Occlusion and perspective may change what each camera can see. The underlying planter may not change to improve either composition independently.
- If image-based production cannot reuse a common 3D object, map the same ordered plant-cluster sequence from the same identifiable seater endpoint in both views. Do not mirror, reseed, redistribute, shorten, or redesign the planting.

## Layer 00 behaviour

- The complete planter and foliage are present as physical architecture in Layer 00.
- All electric planter lighting is off. Add no uplight, rim light, foliage glow, wall shadow effect, emissive strip, or local exposure lift.
- Foliage may receive only the same non-electric residual ambient illumination that affects the surrounding architecture in the approved Layer 00 method. It must remain subdued but physically readable.
- Adding the planter is the only permitted architectural intervention. Camera, crop, perspective, ceiling grid, walls, seater, furniture, materials, colours, products, exposure, and every unrelated element remain locked to the authoritative source.

## Later planter-lighting intent

- Introduce planter lighting only in its explicitly released dedicated cumulative lighting layer.
- Use concealed, integrated 3000K sources within the trough, below the foliage line and fully hidden from both V02 and V06 cameras.
- Aim light upward and slightly toward the rear wall through the foliage. The result should be soft, low-contrast organic leaf shadow play on the cream wall, with gentle depth in the planting.
- Use intermittent concealed source positions coordinated with the calm plant-cluster rhythm. Avoid a continuous luminous line, bright planter rim, hard theatrical gobos, sharp repeated shadows, ceiling spill, global exposure change, or competition with seating comfort and merchandise lighting.
- The physical planter, foliage, and all earlier approved layers remain pixel-frozen when the planter-lighting circuit is added.

## Worker implementation and acceptance checks

Before either V02 or V06 Layer 00 candidate is submitted, verify all of the following:

1. The candidate uses this exact shared planter design and no independent alternative.
2. The trough continuously follows the wall-side curve behind the built-in seater and terminates with that seater.
3. Existing seating architecture, furniture, clearance, camera, crop, perspective, materials, and colours are unchanged.
4. Timber detailing matches the existing seater and no liner, fixture, or construction hardware is visible.
5. Foliage has the same ordered rhythm, height range, density, and species character in both view implementations.
6. Foliage is present but electrically unlit in Layer 00, with no exposure or gamma compensation.
7. The geometry is suitable for the later concealed 3000K foliage-shadow circuit without exposing its source.
8. Cross-view comparison evidence demonstrates one physical design viewed from two cameras, not two separately composed planters.

Any conflict between a view-specific instruction and this shared definition must stop that member lane for control clarification. A worker must not resolve such a conflict by inventing a view-specific planter.
