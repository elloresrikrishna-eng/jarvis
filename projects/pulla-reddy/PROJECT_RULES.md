# G Pulla Reddy Sweets, HSR | Locked Project Rules

This is the control brief for the G Pulla Reddy Sweets lighting presentation being produced in the local Codex project at:

`D:/Codex/G Pulla Reddy Sweets`

The local project structure already exists and must NOT be redesigned or overcomplicated.

## Production folder rules

Keep the existing structure:

- `01_Views/`
- `02_Presentation/`
- `tmp/`
- `tools/`
- `AGENTS.md`
- `README.md`

Every image chat/view gets one numbered view folder. All layers, corrections, alternatives and final images for that view remain directly inside that one folder. Do not create layer subfolders or revision subfolders.

## Absolute visual locks

These rules apply to every view and every layer:

1. Camera position, focal length, perspective, crop and composition are locked.
2. Architecture, ceiling grid, walls, counters, joinery, furniture, materials, colours and product locations are locked unless a specific task explicitly adds a permanent architectural element.
3. Previous lighting layers are frozen. `Layer N = Layer N-1 + exactly one new lighting circuit/effect.`
4. Never globally lift exposure to fake the addition of light. The scene becomes brighter only because a physical lighting layer was added.
5. All lighting is 3000K warm white.
6. No visible pendant lights, tracks, surface spots or decorative luminaires may be added.
7. All ceiling luminaires must read as deep recessed / visually quiet. The fixture should not become a visual object.
8. Light beams, shadows, reflections, glass behaviour and spill created in previous layers must remain materially consistent in later layers.
9. Do not change the ceiling grid to accommodate fixtures. Integrate lighting into it quietly.
10. Final imagery must remain photorealistic and architectural, not stylised or cinematic at the expense of design accuracy.

## Lighting language

### Wall-mounted shelves
All wall-mounted shelves receive BOTH:

- recessed ceiling wall-washer downlights that softly highlight the wall/shelf composition;
- concealed integrated shelf lighting inspired by the user's office display shelf with the Minions. The source must be hidden and the shelf/product should appear gently illuminated, not strip-lit.

### Front sweet display counters
All front display counters receive controlled recessed downlights from above. Prioritise the sweets and product colour while avoiding ugly reflected hot spots in the glass.

### Seating
Where seating is present:

- use restrained recessed downlighting for table/seating comfort where required;
- use concealed under-seater lighting below built-in seaters;
- keep the hospitality zone softer and calmer than the retail counter zone.

### Curved seater planter intervention
For the curved built-in seating zone visible in the source views, add a continuous integrated planter behind the seater as a permanent architectural element.

Rules:

- the planter and foliage must exist from Layer 00 onward whenever that physical seating zone is visible;
- the planter geometry and planting character must remain consistent across every view showing the same seating zone;
- planter lighting is introduced only as its own later lighting layer;
- use concealed/recessed 3000K lighting so foliage throws soft organic shadows onto the background wall;
- avoid theatrical or overly sharp foliage shadows.

## Presentation grammar

Follow the Ashwamedha layered presentation language used in the user's Art Villa workflow and the Purva Bluebelle deck:

- project / section title
- lighting concept sketch for each view
- Layer 00: full night / all electric lighting off
- cumulative layers, one new circuit per layer
- final complete render
- fixture references only where genuinely helpful
- final assembled presentation in `02_Presentation/`

The deck should feel quiet, premium, warm and design-led. It must not look like a generic AI presentation.

## Commercial lighting hierarchy

The visual hierarchy should generally be:

1. sweets / front product display;
2. wall merchandise and shelf displays;
3. central merchandise displays where visible;
4. seating and hospitality comfort;
5. architectural surfaces and material texture;
6. ceiling, which should support the scene rather than dominate it.

## Source-view understanding

The source presentation contains six interior 3D views of the HSR store. The palette is terracotta/red flooring and dado, cream walls, dark timber, brass detailing, white counter tops, glass sweet counters and a strong timber ceiling grid. Preserve that identity. The lighting intervention should enrich it rather than redesign it.
