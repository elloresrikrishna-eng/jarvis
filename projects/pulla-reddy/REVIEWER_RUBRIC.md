# Pulla Reddy | Independent Reviewer Rubric

The reviewer is not Codex and must not grade Codex generously. The job is to challenge the work against design intent, not validate that a file exists.

## Reviewer posture

Assume every candidate may be wrong until evidence shows otherwise.

Do not approve because:
- Codex says QA passed;
- alignment numbers look good;
- the task technically completed;
- the result is merely acceptable.

Approve only when the candidate preserves the locked scene AND materially improves the lighting narrative in the intended way.

## Review dimensions

### 1. Lighting hierarchy
Ask:
- Is the intended hero actually the brightest / most visually compelling object?
- Does the new circuit strengthen the retail story rather than just add illumination?
- Is there enough contrast between retail, circulation and hospitality zones?
- Is the ceiling supporting the scene rather than stealing attention?

Reject if the result is flat, uniformly bright, muddy, over-ambient, or visually confused.

### 2. Fixture invisibility
All sources must feel recessed, concealed or integrated.

Reject if:
- ceiling apertures read as glowing dots;
- fixtures pop visually;
- shelf strips are visible;
- any pendant/track/surface fitting appears;
- the ceiling starts looking perforated or busy.

### 3. Product appetite appeal
For sweet counters and merchandise:
- colours must remain rich and believable;
- food must not look grey, washed-out, over-saturated, plastic or burnt;
- glass must not produce distracting white blobs or mirror-like glare;
- counters must feel premium but not jewellery-store cold.

### 4. 3000K discipline
Reject mixed-CCT contamination unless it is unavoidable residual environmental light from the source. Added electric light must read consistently warm at 3000K.

### 5. Layer purity
Each new layer must introduce only its released lighting family.

Reject if unrelated zones brighten, darken, change contrast, shift colour, or gain new reflections without physical cause.

### 6. Previous-layer freeze
Compare with the approved parent.

Reject if any previous lighting family changes in intensity, beam shape, location, colour, reflection character, or shadow pattern beyond physically unavoidable interaction from the new circuit.

### 7. Camera / architecture integrity
Reject any:
- camera drift;
- focal-length change;
- crop shift;
- warped ceiling grid;
- changed counter geometry;
- shifted shelves;
- moved products/furniture;
- altered materials;
- invented joinery;
- deleted architectural details.

### 8. Beam realism
Downlights should create controlled, believable beam footprints.

Reject if:
- beams look painted on;
- pools are perfectly circular regardless of receiving plane;
- edges are implausibly hard/soft;
- light passes through objects;
- beam directions do not correspond with source position.

### 9. Glass and reflection realism
Check display-counter glass carefully.

Reject obvious bloom, duplicate reflections, fake glowing glass, white clipping, or reflections that move independently of the locked scene.

### 10. Material response
Timber, brass, cream walls, terracotta/red floor, white counters and glass must respond differently to light.

Reject if a new layer simply lifts all RGB values uniformly or makes every surface behave identically.

### 11. Hospitality softness
Seating zones should feel calmer than the sales counters.

Reject if seating becomes as punchy as product display or if under-seater lighting becomes nightclub-like.

### 12. Shelf lighting quality
Wall-mounted shelves require both recessed wall-wash and concealed shelf-integrated light.

Integrated shelf light must:
- hide the source;
- create soft product/shelf glow;
- avoid raw strip lines and LED-dot texture;
- resemble the intended office display-shelf character rather than generic cabinet strip lighting.

### 13. Planter / foliage effect
Where curved seating appears, the planter is persistent architecture from Layer 00 onward.

When planter lighting is introduced:
- foliage shadows must be soft, organic and believable;
- shadow direction must correspond to concealed source position;
- avoid theatrical razor-sharp leaf projections;
- wall must remain readable and calm.

### 14. Exposure continuity
Reject any global exposure or gamma lift masquerading as new light.

The scene gets brighter only where the new physical circuit contributes light or realistic secondary bounce.

### 15. Presentation value
Ask whether the difference between parent and candidate is visually legible enough to deserve its own layer in an Ashwamedha presentation.

If the new effect is too weak to communicate, request stronger but still realistic emphasis. If it overwhelms earlier hierarchy, request reduction.

## Decision outputs

### APPROVE
Use only when:
- all hard locks pass;
- the new lighting family is clear;
- hierarchy improves;
- no important visual defect remains.

### CORRECT
Must include:
1. what specifically fails;
2. where it fails in the image;
3. why it conflicts with the intended design effect;
4. exactly what Codex should change;
5. what must remain frozen while correcting it;
6. measurable/visual success criteria for the next candidate.

Do not write vague feedback such as `make it better`, `too bright`, or `looks off`.

### ESCALATE
Use only for genuine subjective forks where project rules do not establish a clear preference.

## Reviewer standard

The independent reviewer should aim to be more demanding than the design owner during routine production. The purpose of having two different AI systems is useful disagreement and rigorous QA, not mutual confirmation.
